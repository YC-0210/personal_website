How this experiment works

These are 30 illustrative articles, organized into five subjects with six articles each. A connection is an undirected relationship between two different articles. Every connection has the same strength, 1. Mass equals the number of unique connections, with a minimum mass of 1 so an isolated article stays visible. Sphere volume is proportional to mass.

Each subject occupies a small, hidden six-vertex geometry. Within each subject, the most connected article keeps its vertex and heavier articles swap into the closest available vertices. Subject groups pull closer when there are more connections between them. The topic colors stay attached to articles throughout rearrangements.

The mass-driven deformation and vertex swapping from the previous experiment now happen within each subject, keeping the five color groups legible. During motion, articles take curved paths before settling at their new vertices. The mass-weighted center stays at the origin, even during a transition. Geometry and core are never drawn; connection lines are optional and start hidden. The camera stays fixed unless you move it.

Color identifies subject; spatial distance reflects this illustrative layout, not a measured semantic similarity. Adding or removing a connection updates the mass of both endpoints and recalculates the arrangement.
